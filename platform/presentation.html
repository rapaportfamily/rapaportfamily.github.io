The Rapaport Family

Two hundred years, from a rabbi in Galicia to a boy born in Brussels — and everything we were able to prove.

For Dov Rapaport, eighty years old on 28 August 2026

Part oneWhy we made this

For years Dov has said the same thing. What he wanted more than anything was to travel with his mother. To stand with her in the places she came from. To hear her tell the story where it happened, and to learn who his family were.

He cannot. So his family went and did it for him.

Everything here was found slowly, over about a year — in Polish archives, in Yad Vashem, in old birth registers, in passport files, in newspapers eighty years old, and in ninety pages his mother dictated in 1986 when a teacher sat down and asked her.

How we got past Berish. For a whole year the family could go no further back than Berish. Nobody could find his parents — not even a scholar who had spent twenty-five years on Rapaport family history. Then Basia found Berish’s own passport application from 1924, and his parents were written on it: Moses Saul Rapaport and Menycha. One piece of paper moved the family back two generations.

Part fourThe places

All of this happened in real towns. Most of them are in Ukraine today. The names changed when the borders changed.

Nadwórna

Where David was born in 1911, and where his parents married four days earlier. Today it is Nadvirna, in Ukraine.

Bolechów

Where Lusia was born in 1913 and where she married David in 1935. Today it is Bolekhiv.

Stanisławów

The city the family moved to in 1918. David went to school here. Today it is Ivano-Frankivsk.

Lwów

Where Lusia survived the war under a false name, and where David reached her after escaping. Today it is Lviv.

  • 1858

    Three people named Griffel give money to a war fund in Nadwórna. It is the first time this family appears in a newspaper.

    Rabbi Shabtai dies. We know the month because his name stops appearing in the birth register that May.
  • 1886
    Berish Rapaport is born in Radomyśl Wielki. He is Dov’s grandfather.
  • 1888
    Rebeka Griffel is born in Nadwórna. She is Dov’s grandmother.
  • 1904
    Moses Saul pays a notary two crowns so his wife Menycha can collect the post.
  • 1911
    Berish and Rebeka marry on 21 December. Four days later their son David is born.
  • 1912
    Berish is given the right to sign for a Griffel company. The family businesses and the Rapaports are now joined.
  • 1913
    Lusia Weitzner is born in Bolechów. She will become Dov’s mother.
  • 1935
    David and Lusia marry in Bolechów on 10 February.
  • 1937
    Their first son Shimon is born on 22 June.
  • 1939
    The war begins. Everything below happens because of it.
  • 1942
    Berish is taken from the prison in Tarnów to Auschwitz. He dies there on 29 March, two days after arriving. Rebeka is murdered the same year.
  • 1942
    David escapes the Nadwórna ghetto hidden inside a railway wagon, among cut logs.
  • 1942
    Lusia lives in Lwów under a false name. She hides four-year-old Shimon in a wooden crate against the wall.
  • 1942
    David’s sister Lotte buys a forged American paper for ten thousand dollars. The sellers report her to the Gestapo. She never comes back.
  • 1945
    The war ends. On 26 December Lusia registers in Brussels as a displaced person — someone with no home to go back to.
  • 1946
    Dov is born in Brussels on 28 August, at 250 rue des Coteaux.
  • 1947
    David and Shimon sail for Palestine first. The boats will not take mothers with babies, and Dov is a few months old.
  • 1948
    The family is together again, in Haifa.
  • 2015
    Dov starts going back. Warsaw in April, then Przemyśl.
  • 2018
    Dov visits Brussels, where he was born.
  • 2019
    Dov visits Katowice. He has now been to four of the places.
  • 2026
    This archive is finished for his eightieth birthday.

Part sixThe papers that prove it

An archive is only worth as much as the paper behind it. Here are some of the documents, and what each one tells us.

A birth, written down in 1911

The certificate with his face on it

The record that says how he died

Berish was not taken from a ghetto. He was taken from the prison in Tarnów, on a transport of fifty-five men. He was given the number 27310. He arrived on 27 March 1942 and died two days later.

The card his son signed in 1953

For a long time this archive said that nobody had ever filled in a memorial page for Berish and Rebeka. That was wrong. David filled them in himself, in Haifa, on 26 April 1953. Both pages were in the archive the whole time. When we found the mistake we corrected it, and we left the correction on the website where everyone can see it.

Her own handwriting, Brussels, 1945

A displaced person was somebody who had survived the war but had no home to go back to. Lusia registered as one in Brussels on 26 December 1945 and signed the card herself.

Something strange. Her card says she was born in Haifa. She was born in Bolechów. David’s card says he was born in Cieszyn; he was born in Nadwórna. Both cards give the wrong birthplace. Nobody knows why. We wrote the question down instead of inventing an answer.

A list of names, and a man trying to save his family

This torn page sat in the archive for ten days labelled as somebody’s portrait. It is not a portrait. It is a list of fifteen families, with an address written at the bottom in pencil: care of Jakob Griffel, in Bucharest. He was a relative who worked on rescue during the war. Six of the fifteen names on the list are his own brothers and sisters.

David got out of the ghetto by hiding inside a railway wagon. A neighbour drilled holes into thick logs that were being loaded, and eighteen men hid among them. Somebody informed on them, so they jumped off the moving train. He reached Lwów on foot.

Lusia lived in Lwów under a borrowed name, Maria Cizlik. She ran an antiques shop. She kept Shimon, who was four, hidden inside a narrow wooden crate standing against the wall — the kind people used for storing potatoes.

"Do something so that I am not a Jew," Shimon used to ask his mother. He was four years old.

Part eightDov went back

He could not take his mother. So he went himself, four times, and photographed it.

Brussels is where he was born. He stood in the city again seventy-two years later.

Part nineThe biggest things we found

Two generations further back

Berish’s passport application named his parents. The family line now reaches back to a rabbi born in 1823.

The day Berish died

Not just the year. 29 March 1942, confirmed by three separate archives — and the prison he was taken from.

A rabbi’s death date, with no death record

At a circumcision, one man holds the baby. The register writes his name down every time. Rabbi Shabtai’s name stops in April 1884, and his son stands in for him three weeks later. That is how we know when he died.

What happened to Lotte

The family knew she was killed but not how. The answer was in Lusia’s own memoir all along, on page 40. She bought a forged American paper. The sellers reported her.

Eighty years in the newspapers

One hundred and forty-two press notices about the family, from 1866 to 1946 — a shop, an oil refinery, a sawmill, a bankruptcy, a trial.

Berish’s name, twice, in print

In 1912 he is given the right to sign for a family company. In 1913 a drilling journal reports a new business called "Griffel jun. et Rappaport".

Lusia was in Brussels earlier than anyone thought

By December 1945 — three and a half months before David got there.

The questions we could not answer

They are written down too. An archive that hides what it does not know cannot be trusted about what it does.

LastOne rule

Nothing here was invented. Where two sources disagree, we show both. Where we do not know, we say we do not know — because the people who would have kept those records are the people who were killed, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of forgetting.
