# The story
Ten chapters. Each paragraph is followed by the records it rests on.

## 0. Before the beginning — Radomyśl Wielki  
*1850—1886*

For a year this family could go back no further than a name on a passport. The paternal line stopped at Berisz Rapaport, and even Edward Gelles, who spent twenty-five years on the Rapaport rabbinical genealogies, could not carry it past him. In May 2026 Basia found the passport file itself, and inside it were his parents: Moses Saul Rapaport and Menukha.

> Sources: Berisz Rapaport passport application, Stanisławów 1924

Berisz was born on 30 July 1886 in Radomyśl Wielki, and the family's registered commune was Dąbrowa Tarnowska — a town nobody could account for until July 2026, when Basia read the Tarnów notarial records and found Szabse Rapaport serving as rabbi of Dąbrowa until 20 April 1884. That is why the passport says Dąbrowa. The same evening, from a completely different direction, Dana was sent a record naming Schabse with Figa/Yete.

> Sources: Tarnów notarial records (Basia, July 2026); MyHeritage record, 7 July 2026

Three marriage deeds from Tarnów — 1919, 1920 and 1923, for three of Berisz's sisters — each independently name the same couple as their parents. Three separate clerks, in three different years, writing the same two names. That is as close to certainty as this kind of research reaches.

> Sources: Tarnów marriage registers 1919 (entry 40), 1920 (entry 98), 1923 (entry 101)

Behind all of it now stands a spine of paper. Fifty-eight civil-register photographs came from Basia's archive on 6 August 2026 — twenty-seven births, eleven marriages, nineteen deaths — running from a birth in 1818 to a death in 1934. They are the ordinary machinery of a town: a clerk writing down that a child was born, that two people married, that someone died. Read together they carry six generations of this family from Abraham Abe, born 1784, down to the parents of the man this archive is written for. The naming was tested before any of it was believed: twenty-seven of the twenty-eight birth records carry the same year this archive already held for that person. One does not — a birth filed 1849 where we hold 1850 — and it is published as the disagreement it is.

> Sources: 58 civil-register photographs, Basia’s family archive, received 6 August 2026

And then, on 19 October 1904, Moses Saul walked into a notary's office in Tarnów and paid two crowns. Entry 392 of the day-book: Mojżesz Rapaport, capitalist in Tarnów, legalises his signature on a power of attorney so that his wife Menicha may collect the post. That is the whole of it. No catastrophe, no register of the dead — a married couple arranging who fetches the mail. It is the earliest ordinary day this archive holds from the direct line, and it is the only piece of paper anywhere in it that names Menycha Leiner in her own right. She had been a name on a tree with no birth date, no death date and nothing of her own. Here she is, with legal authority over her husband's letters.

> Sources: Tarnów notarial repertory, entry 392, 19 October 1904 — held in this archive as doc_tarnow_notary_1904_postal_poa; Transcribed and translated by Basia, sent 20 August 2026; Moses Saul Rapaport (1858 – Tarnów, 8 November 1933) and Menycha Leiner, extended_tree I1 and I2

## 1. Nadwórna — David  
*1911*

David Mendel Rapaport — Memek — was born in Nadwórna on 25 December 1911, son of Berisz Rapaport and Rebeka Griffel. Nadwórna sat in the Carpathian foothills of eastern Galicia, then Austria-Hungary, then Poland, today Nadvirna in Ukraine.

> Sources: Nadwórna civil birth register

His Brussels Displaced Persons card of 1946 gives his birthplace as "Cieszyn (Tesin)" — a town four hundred kilometres away, where no one in this family ever lived. It is a transcription error by a French clerk, and it is worth pausing on: here the document is wrong and the family's memory is right. That is why nothing in this archive ranks paper above testimony as a rule.

> Sources: DIPIS card, Brussels 1946; Nadwórna civil birth register

## 2. Bolechów — Lusia  
*1913—1933*

Leah Weitzner — Lusia — was born in Bolechów on 8 April, daughter of Elias (Eliyahu) Weitzner and Matel (Matylda) née Weinreb. Every record agrees on the day. They do not agree on the year: the Bolechów civil register says 1913, while her own survivor card, the ŻIH index and the Haifa burial register all say 1916. Three against two, but the two include the civil register. It remains unresolved.

> Sources: Bolechów birth register 1913; CKŻP card nr 151738; Haifa Chevra Kadisha register

In 1933 her parents left for Mandatory Palestine. Lusia stayed. It is a small sentence with the whole of what follows inside it: she would not see them again for fifteen years, and the war would fall on the side of the family that remained.

> Sources: Memoir ch.1 — family testimony, no document held

## 3. The marriage, and Shimon  
*1935—1938*

They married on 10 February 1935 in Bolechów, her own town. She was twenty-one and he was twenty-three.

> Sources: Civil marriage record, Bolechów, 10 February 1935

Her memoir puts the wedding in Muszyna, in February 1938 — three years later and in another town. Written forty years afterwards, the memory placed the marriage at the opening of the Muszyna years rather than three years before them. The month is right in both. And the record resolves something the memoir left broken: under her date, Shimon arrives eight months before his parents marry; under the register, he is born two and a half years into the marriage.

> Sources: Memoir p.16; Civil marriage record 1935

Shimon was born on 22 June 1937 in Lwów. The family had long said "Mosina" — a name that turned out to be Muszyna — but his own survivor card, filled in after the war, says Lwów.

> Sources: CKŻP survivor card nr 151337

## 4. The war  
*1939—1944*

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939. What the family did over the next five years is known almost entirely from Lusia's testimony, because almost no one who could have written it down survived to do so.

> Sources: Memoir chs.2-6

She tells of Przemyśl under Soviet administration; of the Nadwórna ghetto and David's escape from it hidden in a wooden wagon; of false identities in Lwów; of the Hormak family, and of neighbours named Pietrozycki and Ivanov who helped them; and of Shimon, three years old, kept in a crate for two and a half years so that he would not be seen or heard. No document we hold corroborates any of it. That is not a weakness in the account — it is what happens when the people who would have kept the records are the people who were killed.

> Sources: Memoir chs.3-6 — no corroborating record held

Two things from those years are on paper. David was stripped of Polish citizenship in 1942. And his father Berisz was murdered at Auschwitz — not on an approximate date but on a known one. He was given prisoner number 27310, arrived at the camp on 27 March 1942 and was dead on 29 March. Two days. Two independent records say so and agree exactly: the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum's victim page, and the camp's own Sterbebücher, the death books kept for registered prisoners. The register adds one word that matters — his residence, Przemyśl. Lusia's memoir, written from memory decades later, says David's parents were in Przemyśl when the war came and that the Germans killed one of them there. A German clerk writing at the time put him in the same town. Neither could have copied the other. The camp recorded his occupation as robotnik, labourer; he was an industrialist in Stanisławów, and that word is the camp's, not his. And we now know where he was taken from. His transport, number 689, was fifty-five men from the PRISON IN TARNÓW, registered together on 27 March 1942 with numbers 27305 to 27359 and sent by the Security Police of the Kraków district; Berisz was the sixth number in that block. Eighteen of the fifty-five were recorded as Jewish and seventeen of them died; at least forty-one of the fifty-five did. So he was not seized in Przemyśl and sent east — he was held in Tarnów, a short way from Radomyśl Wielki where he was born, in the town whose address the family still carried in 1924: Folwarczna 9.

> Sources: Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum victim page (prisoner 27310); Auschwitz Sterbebücher 1941–1943, source record 49478; Lusia’s memoir, ch. 24; DIPIS card, Brussels 1946; Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum, transport 689 — read at source 6 August 2026

And they were remembered. For a long time this page said that no Page of Testimony had ever been filed at Yad Vashem for Berisz or for Rebeka, and that this archive was their only written memorial anywhere. That was wrong — and not because the evidence was missing. Both pages were already here, attached to their own records in this archive's document list: No. 53072 for Bernard, No. 53074 for Regina, each signed on 26 April 1953. The story simply had not been read against the file it was built on. Basia's copies, which arrived on 6 August 2026, are what finally prompted the check. Berisz's page names his father Moshe and his mother Menucha, gives his trade as merchant and sets his death in 1940 — eleven years after the fact, and two years early. Rebeka's gives her birth as 1892 in Nadwórna and her death as 1943; this archive holds 1888 and 1942. Those dates disagree and are left disagreeing. What does not disagree is that in the spring of 1953 somebody who had known them sat down and wrote their names on a form so they would not be lost. They were never unremembered. We had simply not looked at what we were holding. And now we know whose hand. The declaration at the foot of both pages is a filer living in Haifa, signing on 26 April 1953, and where the form asks what he was to the person it says בנו on his father’s page and בנה on his mother’s — his son, her son. Berish and Rebeka had one son. David wrote them. Seven years after he came ashore he sat down and filled in two forms for his parents, and put his father’s death two years wrong, because he was writing from memory about people he had last seen before the war.

> Sources: Yad Vashem Page of Testimony 53072 (Bernard Rapaport), signed 26 April 1953 — held in this archive as doc_bernard_dov_yv_pot; Yad Vashem Page of Testimony 53074 (Regina Rapaport), signed 26 April 1953 — held in this archive as doc_regina_rivka_yv_pot; The submitter block of both Pages of Testimony, read 11 August 2026

There is one more sheet, and for ten days this archive had it filed as a photograph of a face. It is a torn page of German typing: fifteen numbered families, with birth dates and street addresses in Kraków, Sosnowiec, Bielsko and Lublin, and at the foot, in pencil, an address in Bucharest — care of Ing. L. Filderman, Strada Paleologu — for Jakob Griffel. Dr Jacob Griffel was the Agudath Israel and Vaad Hatzalah rescue representative in Istanbul, one of the few channels through which Orthodox rescue money and papers reached occupied Europe. When Basia showed the sheet to Ms Manowska of the Pilecki Institute, who researches the wartime 'passports of life', she recognised it at sight as belonging to the operation that issued foreign passports to Jews, and asked at once where it had been found and what its reference number was. Six of the fifteen entries are the brothers and sisters of David's own family — the children of Isaac Chaim Griffel — and the birth dates match this family's tree to the month. This was a man in Bucharest trying to get his own relatives onto paper that might count as a passport. Of the people on that sheet, Samuel lived and died in Brooklyn in 1999; Osias Heinrich lived and died in Jersey City in 1968; Menashe Karmel lived until 1989. Schalom, his wife and both of his little daughters were murdered in 1942. Schmul Rosenbojm was murdered at Lublin in 1942 and his son Izajasz in 1943. Whether a single passport was ever issued against this list, and whether it reached anybody in time, is not something this archive can say.

> Sources: The sheet itself — held in this archive as doc_griffel_rescue_list_bucharest, received from Jacob with the Griffel family library on 10 August 2026; Ms Manowska, Pilecki Institute, reported by Basia on 19 August 2026; Cross-checked line by line against griffel_tree.json (390 people) and against the Pilecki Institute's published Ładoś List, 2,987 entries parsed in full

## 5. Katowice — and a separation nobody recorded  
*1945—1946*

The war ended and they came west with the repatriation, to Katowice. On 1 July 1946 Lusia and Shimon each filed a survivor-registration card at the Katowice committee — cards 151738 and 151337, mother and son, one after the other.

> Sources: CKŻP cards nr 151738 and nr 151337, Katowice, 1 July 1946

But David had been registered as a Displaced Person in Brussels since 9 April 1946 — three months earlier. On the day he was recorded in Belgium, his wife and son were still in Poland. He had gone ahead, and they followed later that year. The memoir says only that "the family reaches Brussels and stays in a hotel": one movement, everyone together. The two cards, held in this archive since May and never read against each other until August 2026, say otherwise. It is the first of the two separations in this story.

> Sources: DIPIS card, 9 April 1946; CKŻP cards, 1 July 1946; Memoir p.62

## 6. Brussels — and Dov  
*1946*

And she was there first. For as long as this archive has existed, the earliest Brussels record in it was David’s Displaced Persons card of 9 April 1946. On 19 August 2026 Basia sent Lusia’s: she registered in the same city on 26 DECEMBER 1945, three and a half months earlier, signing it "Rapaport Lea" while the clerk filed her under her maiden name. Two days later a Jewish aid society asked the Belgians to let her stay six months; on 7 February 1946 the Ministry of Justice agreed, on condition the permission was marked "with a view to emigration" — she could be in Belgium only while waiting to leave it. She was living at 250 rue des Coteaux in Schaerbeek. Dov was born under that arrangement. Her card carries two things nobody can yet explain: it says she came from a camp in Prague, which appears nowhere else and not in her own book; and it gives her birthplace as Haifa, Palestine. She was born in Bolechów. David’s card of the same series names Cieszyn, also wrong. Two cards, two wrong birthplaces — and on hers, under destination, the word Palestine.

> Sources: doc_lusia_1945_dp_card; doc_lusia_1945_residence_request; doc_lusia_1945_stay_permit_1946; doc_david_dp_card

David's Displaced Persons card describes a forestry engineer, Polish nationality stripped in 1942, speaking Polish, Italian, German, Czech and Russian. It is the only document that reaches across from the war into what came after.

> Sources: DIPIS card, Brussels, 9 April 1946

Dov Bernard Rapaport was born in Brussels on 28 August 1946. No Belgian birth registration is held in this archive — it is the easiest missing document of all, and only he can request it, as the person the record belongs to.

> Sources: Family (Doron, 2026); Memoir p.62

One thing about Belgium had been sitting in plain sight. David's aunt Jente — his father's sister — spent the war in Liège, at Rue Lairesse 88, registered as a Polish national. Her daughter Szewa married there in 1941, a marriage this archive already held without seeing what stood behind it. So when David and Lusia reached Belgium in 1946, it was not a country the family had never been to: part of it had lived there straight through the occupation, a hundred kilometres from Brussels. Whether David went where he had people, no document in our hands says. It is worth asking and it is not worth inventing.

> Sources: Wartime name record 4494611 (Jenta Rapaport, Rue Lairesse 88, Liège); Liège marriage of Szewa Horowitz and Markus Türkel, 1941

## 7. Sète, the Theodor Herzl, Cyprus  
*1947—1948*

On 2 April 1947 David and Shimon sailed from Sète in southern France aboard the Theodor Herzl — the ex-British cable-layer HMS Guardian, built at Newcastle in 1907, carrying 2,641 ma'apilim gathered from camps across France and Belgium. Shimon was nine. Lusia stayed behind in Brussels with the seven-month-old Dov.

> Sources: Memoir p.63; Palyam / ship histories — the vessel is fully documented

On 13 April the Royal Navy boarded her off the coast. The passengers fought; three were killed and twenty-seven wounded. Everyone aboard was deported to Cyprus, and David and Shimon were interned at Karaolos, north of Famagusta, for about eight months. The camps there held 53,510 Jews behind wire between 1946 and 1949, and some two thousand children were born inside them. Shimon was released from Atlit in 1948.

> Sources: Memoir pp.63-66; Cyprus internment camp figures, 1946-1949

## 8. Haifa  
*1948—1996*

Lusia sailed from Brussels with Dov on the Kadima. She had waited more than a year alone with the baby, working, while David and Shimon were behind wire. The Haifa burial register records her year of immigration as 1948 and David's as 1946 — the two-year gap the memoir describes, written into their own graves. Dov's first journey was with his mother, the two of them.

> Sources: Memoir p.66; Haifa Chevra Kadisha register

In Haifa she saw her parents for the first time since 1933. The family settled on Abbas Street, and later bought an apartment on Moriah Street with German reparations — money David argued with himself about taking. He worked as a customs broker, the trade his son Dov followed, and then his grandson Doron.

> Sources: Memoir pp.67-76

In 1986 a granddaughter, Hadas, asked her teacher Esther Weiss for help with a school assignment, and Esther sat down and interviewed Lusia. Ninety pages in Hebrew. Without them, almost nothing in this archive between 1939 and 1948 would have a voice at all.

> Sources: The memoir, compiled 1986

David died on 29 August 1990 and Lusia on 28 December 1996. They are buried beside one another at Sde Yehoshua, graves 102ד and 102ג. Her burial record names her father Eliyahu and her mother Matilda — the same two people her memoir opens with, sixty years earlier and in another language. The last page of the record matches the first page of the testimony.

> Sources: Haifa Chevra Kadisha register; Memoir ch.1

## 9. Going back  
*2015 – 2019*

In April 2015 Dov flew to Poland with his son Doron. They spent a week in Warsaw — the Old Town, the Barbican, the Royal Castle, a city flattened and put back stone by stone — and on 19 April they drove to Przemyśl. That is not a sightseeing stop. Przemyśl is the town where Berish and Rebeka were living when the war came; it is the residence the Auschwitz death books give for Berish, and it is where Lusia’s memoir says the Germans killed one of David’s parents on a fabricated charge. Most of the forty photographs from that day are taken through a car windscreen, driving its streets slowly, in the rain.

> Sources: Dov and Doron, Warsaw and Przemyśl albums, April 2015; Auschwitz Sterbebücher — residence Przemyśl; Lusia’s memoir, ch. 24

In June 2018 he went to Brussels, where he was born on 28 August 1946 — four months after his father filled in a Displaced Persons card in that city and wrote "Palestine or U.S.A." in the box for where he wanted to go. In January 2019 he went to Katowice, in the snow, and photographed it from above and on foot: the town of the separation nobody recorded, where the family was together before David and Shimon went one way and Lusia and the baby another. One hundred and twenty photographs from the four journeys are in this archive.

> Sources: Dov and Doron, Brussels and Katowice albums, 2018 and 2019; DIPIS card, Brussels, 9 April 1946

It is worth saying what that means for an archive like this one. Every other chapter here is assembled from what other people wrote down — clerks, registrars, a camp administration, a memoir. This is the only chapter in which somebody in the story goes back and looks for himself, and the pictures are his. Nothing in them was recovered. They were made.

> Sources: doc_dov_journeys_back

- Died: 1996-12-28 pl_haifa

**nickname_lajka** — Her nickname was Lajka. The memoir, page 9, says her father called her wild — “like a demon”, “like a naughty boy” — but Lajka was what she was called, and she never knew what it meant. The one pre-war photograph of her carries that name as its caption: “Lajka on the horse”.

**marriage** — Married David Rapaport on 10 February 1935 in Bolechów, per the civil marriage record. Her memoir places the wedding in Muszyna in February 1938 — three years later and in another town; the month is right in both. Conflict recorded, not resolved.

**marriage_age_gap** — She was 21 and he was 23 at the marriage of February 1935. The memoir says 18 and 24, which follows from its own later date.

**fake_identity** — Lived in Lwów under fake identity 'Maria Cizlik' (borrowed)

**lwow_residence** — Legionów Street 24, Lwów (with Hormek fake documents) — opposite the theater

**lwow_job** — Manager of art-effects/antiques store under fake identity, ~1941

**przemysl_job** — Briefly worked as teller / in the passport section in Przemyśl, 1939, during the Soviet occupation

**1945_destination_affidavit** — Received an affidavit to emigrate to the USA (per Doron 17 May)

**1945_46_polish_records** — Appears in Polish post-war records 1945-46; not yet located in Brussels 1946 DP records under any spelling. Likely traveled separately from David before they reunited in Brussels for Dov's birth.

**muszyna_job** — Managed a hotel in Muszyna (the resort town) where 'all the social cream' of the town's visitors stayed

**1936_lwow** — In 1936 Lusia moved to Lwow to live with well-off relatives who owned a pharmacy. The purpose of the move was to learn a trade: she was enrolled at a professional dressmaking school, “Kleftinowa”, one of the prestigious schools in the city. Memoir p.15. This replaces an earlier garbled entry that read “attended Carpathian forestry school filings” — which the memoir does not say and which was not traceable to any source.

**memoir** — Wrote memoir of the war (Hebrew)

**burial** — Sde Yehoshua cemetery (Kfar Samir), Haifa — Section ו, Row 22, Grave 102ג (adjacent to David)

## Auschwitz transport 689 — 55 men from the prison in Tarnów, 27 March 1942

- Type: deportation_transport
- Source: Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum, Database of victims — transport 689. Read at source on the open web, 6 August 2026.
- People: Berisz / Bernard / Dov Rapaport
- Online: https://victims.auschwitz.org/transports/689
- Online: https://victims.auschwitz.org/victims/188161

The transport that carried David’s father to Auschwitz, read from the Museum’s own database rather than from a copy. Fifty-five men from the prison in Tarnów, registered at Auschwitz on 27 March 1942 with numbers 27305 to 27359, sent by the Security Police (Sicherheitspolizei) and Security Service (Sicherheitsdienst) of the Kraków district. The Museum records that no documentation survives explaining the circumstances of the transport’s arrival and admission. Eighteen of the fifty-five were identified in the camp records as Jewish, and seventeen of those died at Auschwitz; at least forty-one of the fifty-five died there. Berish Rapaport held number 27310 — the sixth in the block — and died two days after arrival, on 29 March 1942. The detail that changes the story is where the transport started: the prison in Tarnów. The family came from Radomyśl Wielki, a short way from Tarnów, and their registered address in 1924 was Tarnów, Folwarczna 9. He was not taken from Przemyśl and sent east; he was held in the town his family belonged to.

Birthplace of the documented medieval Rapapa ancestors. The 1305 conspiracy to rescue a Jewish girl from forced conversion-by-marriage was led here by Dr Vidal Rapapa. Court physician Dr Jucef Salomon Rapapa served King Jaime III here. DNA testing confirmed living Mallorcan Chueta family ↔ US Rappoport family direct mitochondrial link.


## pl_portobuffole

Where R. Yechiel Michael ha-Kohen Rapa was born in 1502. His son Isaac 'HaMoel' was the first to use the surname 'Rapa-Porto' (~1550), creating the modern Rapaport name.


## pl_verona

Where R. Avraham Menachem ha-Kohen Rapa-Porto published Mincha Belulah in 1594, introducing the raven + priestly blessing family emblem still used by descendants today.


## pl_padua

Where the Katzenellenbogen rabbinical dynasty was founded. MaHaRaM Padua served as Chief Rabbi of Padua until 1565.


## pl_brest

Home of Saul Wahl Katzenellenbogen (the legendary 'King for one night' of Poland) and his rabbinical descendants.


## pl_tarnobrzeg

Where Chawa Wahl (1877-1941), David Mendel Griffel's wife, was born — into the Tarnobrzeg Wahl line documented as descending from Saul Wahl Katzenellenbogen.


## pl_stanislawow

Birthplace of Dr Chanan Rapaport (1928-2022), the Rapaport family genealogist. Regional capital of the voivodeship containing Nadwórna. Two known Rapaport households here in the 1920s — Berisz at Kościuszki 4 (1924), and a Chaim Samuel Rapaport family from 1916 (cousin candidate).


## pl_radomysl_wielki

BIRTHPLACE of Berisz Rapaport (1886, the family patriarch's son who married Rebeka Griffel) and his siblings Alte Leja (1882), Jente (1887), Freida Amalia (1888) and the younger Rebeka (1896). Source: family-built descendants tree of Moses Saul Rapaport + 1924 passport application (USHMM RG-31.064M) which gives Berisz's birthplace as Radomyśl Wielki. Notable for the unusually high proportion of Jewish residents — Radomyśl produced multiple famous figures including: Eliezer Margoshes (writer/historian, descendant of Solomon Luria); Reuven Iceland (Yiddish/Hebrew poet); Samuel Margoshes (Zionist Organization of America chairman); also reportedly the paternal ancestors of Arthur Miller and grandparents of Steven Spielberg.


## pl_auschwitz

Where Berisz / Bernard Dov Rapaport (paternal great-grandfather) was murdered. Confirmed by Auschwitz Museum victim database record #188161, transport #689. URL: https://victims.auschwitz.org/victims/188161. His sister Alte Leja Rapaport (m. Turkel) was also murdered at Auschwitz 1942.


## pl_olchowce

Site of the 1908 religious marriage of Berisz Rapaport × Rebeka Griffel per Edward Gelles's Griffel pedigree. The civil marriage was registered on 21 December 1911 in Nadwórna; the religious wedding (kiddushin) at Olchowce three years earlier is consistent with Jewish-Galician practice where ritual and civil marriages were sometimes separated by years.

This archive publishes disagreements rather than resolving them quietly. A film should show them as disagreements. **The open questions below are questions, not findings — a film that answers them invents history.**

# How this was found — and where we were wrong

The archive began with one memoir. Lusia wrote her life down. Everything else was found.

**Basia**, a genealogist in Poland, rebuilt the Rapaport line back to Abraham Abe, born 1784 at
Tarnów. On 5 August 2026 she sent the tree as a GEDCOM — 1,442 people, 480 families — and
mentioned in passing that the printed version is five metres long. On 6 August she sent her
entire media library: 1,072 files, 1.1 GB.

**Jacob** sent the other side of the house — the GRIFFEL line, David's mother's family, 390
people and 569 files. Not one of his files appears in Basia's. Two researchers, two families,
meeting at one woman: Rebeka Griffel. Because of him, David's maternal grandparents have names
for the first time: Lajzor Griffel, born 26 March 1850 at Nadwórna, and Sara Matel Chajes, born
5 March 1851, who lived into the first year of the occupation.

**Ms. Kasia** at the Jewish Historical Institute in Warsaw found David's birth certificate in
Nadwórna on 15 May 2026, correcting a birthplace a Brussels clerk had misheard in 1946.

**The open internet** did the rest. The Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial's own database confirmed
Berish's prisoner number and death date at source — and its transport page gave us something
nobody had: transport 689 was fifty-five men **from the prison at Tarnów**, so he was not seized
in Przemyśl and sent east; he was held in the town his family came from.

## Where we were wrong

This matters as much as what we found.

- The archive stated for months that **no Page of Testimony had ever been filed** for Berish or
  Rebeka, and that it was their only memorial anywhere. Both pages were sitting in its own
  document list the whole time. Worse and better: **David filed them himself**, from Haifa, on
  26 April 1953 — three of them, for his father, his mother and his sister Lotte, signing each
  in the relationship line as *his son, her son, her brother*.
- A 1924 certificate was filed as belonging to "a different family" because a second page in
  the same record was a different man's birth entry. The certificate itself names *Mendel 12
  lat, Lotte 8 lat* — David and his sister. Reopened, with both readings kept.
- Two Charlottes, born in Vienna a year apart, first cousins, were nearly merged into one.

## What is still missing

Two things, and the site names them both: `Photobook2.pdf`, whose 47 pages are already
published one by one, and *"Lea and Shimon .pdf"*, a document catalogued from correspondence
whose scan has never reached us.

# FILM — what is new, and what must not be missed

Read this before writing a script. These are the records the family has flagged as carrying the most weight.


## [HIGH] Typed list of fifteen families, addressed care of Jakob Griffel in Bucharest — identified by the Pilecki Institute as belonging to the wartime operation to issue foreign passports to Jews

**If the film shows one document large and reads it out loud, this is the one. Fifteen numbered lines, six of them the brothers and sisters of David’s own family, and a man in Bucharest trying to get them onto paper that might count as a foreign passport. Then say which of them lived.**

- Source: Jacob, Griffel family archive, received 10 August 2026. Identified by Ms Manowska of the Pilecki Institute, reported by Basia on 19 August 2026.
- Scan: https://rapaportfamily.github.io/assets/documents/griffel_2026_08/photos/I16__jacob_griffel.jpg
- What it records: language = German, typewritten, torn along the right edge — the right-hand third of every line is missing; entries = 15 numbered, a pencilled "16 =" below; marked_with_X = entries 6, 8 and 9 carry a pencilled X. Meaning unknown.; forwarding_address = Jakob Griffel, c/o Ing. L. Filderman, Strada Paleolog[u], Bucureşti; who_jacob_griffel_was = Dr Jacob Griffel (1900-1962), the Agudath Israel and Vaad Hatzalah rescue representative in Istanbul — the channel through which Orthodox rescue money and papers moved into occupied Europe; who_filderman_was = the address places the sheet with the Bucharest end of that channel; Wilhelm Filderman led Romanian Jewry, and the pencil reads "Ing. L. Filderman"

A torn sheet of typing that this archive misfiled for ten days as a portrait of Dr Jacob Griffel, because the file was named after him. It is not a portrait. It is a list of fifteen families — names, birth dates, street addresses in Kraków, Sosnowiec, Bielsko and Lublin — with his own forwarding address pencilled at the foot: care of Ing. L. Filderman, Strada Paleologu, Bucharest. Jacob Griffel was the Agudath Israel and Vaad Hatzalah rescue man in Istanbul. Shown this sheet, Ms Manowska of the Pilecki Institute — who researches the "passports of life" — recognised at sight that it came from the operation that issued foreign passports to Jews, and asked immediately where it had been found and what its reference number was.

Six of the fifteen entries are the children of Isaac Chaim Griffel and Judith Breit, and the birth dates match this family’s tree to the month: Dawid Mendel b. 1902 with his wife Laja and their daughter Rebeka Rachela; Schalom b. 1904 with his wife and two little girls; Samuel b. 1913; Amalia b. 1912; Rebeka b. July 1919; Osias Heinrich b. 1910. Entry 15 is their sister Teofila with her husband Rosenbojm and their small son. This was a man in Bucharest trying to get his own family onto paper that might save them.

And then the outcome, which the film must not soften. Samuel lived, and died in Brooklyn in 1999. Osias Heinrich lived, and died in Jersey City in 1968. Menashe Karmel, entry 6, lived until 1989. Schalom, his wife and both daughters were murdered in 1942. Schmul Rosenbojm was murdered in Lublin in 1942 and his son Izajasz in 1943. Zalman Horowitz died in 1945. Whether any passport was ever issued against this sheet, and whether it reached anybody, this archive cannot say.

> Still unknown: What is the reference number and provenance of this sheet? Ms Manowska asked first and it is still unanswered — Jacob sent the file on 10 August with the rest of the Griffel library.

> Still unknown: Who put a pencilled X beside entries 6, 8 and 9 — and did it mean saved, refused, or already dead?

> Still unknown: The last line ends with a dash and the word "Eisig". Chaim Eiss of Zurich was one of the six men of the Ładoś Group who made these passports; "Eisig Chaim" is also a given name that runs through this very family. It could be either. Do not decide it in a film.

> Still unknown: Are Minna and Toni at entry 11 the same two girls the tree calls Chaja Mindel and Thea? Both were murdered in 1942 either way.

- Scan: https://rapaportfamily.github.io/assets/documents/basia_2026_08_19/lusia_dp_card_1945_back.jpg
- What it records: annotation_top_left = ép.se RAPAPORT — wife of Rapaport; family_name = WEITZNER; given_name = Léa; signature_of_registrant = Rapaport Lea; claimed_nationality = Polonaise; religion = Israélite; birthdate_as_written = 8.4.1916; birthplace_as_written = Haifa, Palestine — SHE WAS BORN IN BOLECHÓW; father = Elias; mother_maiden_name = Mathilde Weinreb; desired_destination = Palestine; last_residence_1_january_1938 = Lemberg (Lwów), Pologne; languages_in_order = German, Polish, Russian; prisoner_of_war = No; date = 26.12.45; assembly_center = Bru.; remarks = Vient de Prague — se rend en Palestine  (Comes from Prague — going to Palestine)

Lusia’s own card, the twin of David’s. She registered in Brussels on 26 December 1945 — three and a half months BEFORE he did on 9 April 1946, and eight months before Dov was born there. She signed it Rapaport Lea while the clerk filed her under her maiden name and added "ép.se Rapaport" at the top. Three things it gives us. Her parents: father Elias, mother’s maiden name MATHILDE WEINREB — the Weitzner–Weinreb line. The remark "Vient de Prague" — she came from a camp in Prague, which nothing else in this archive mentions and which is now an open question. And a birthplace that is simply wrong: HAIFA, PALESTINE. She was born in Bolechów. David’s card of the same series gives Cieszyn, also wrong. Two cards, two wrong birthplaces — and on Lusia’s the desired destination is Palestine. The archive records the pattern and asserts no motive.

> Still unknown: WHICH camp in Prague, and when was she there? Nothing else in this archive mentions Prague at all.

> Still unknown: Why is her birthplace given as Haifa? Her stated destination on the same card is Palestine. No source states a reason.


## [HIGH] Request for temporary residence in Belgium, 28 December 1945

**FILM — USE THIS. The strongest new evidence in the archive, and it changes the order of chapter 5: Lusia reached Brussels three and a half months before David.**

- Source: Basia, received 19 August 2026. Transcribed and translated by Basia.
- Scan: https://rapaportfamily.github.io/assets/documents/basia_2026_08_19/lusia_residence_request_1945-12-28.pdf
- What it records: body = International Refugee Committee, Belgian Branch; submitted_by = the association "Aid to Jewish Victims of War"; on_behalf_of = Lea Weitzner, born Haifa 8.04.1916, Polish; residence_abroad = Lwów; address_in_belgium = 250 rue des Coteaux, Schaerbeek; reason_for_stay = came from a camp in Prague, awaiting departure for Palestine; duration_requested = 6 months; dated = Brussels, 28 December 1945

Two days after she registered as a displaced person, a Jewish aid society asked the Belgian authorities to let her stay six months. It repeats the two strange things from her card — born Haifa, came from a camp in Prague — and adds an address: 250 rue des Coteaux, Schaerbeek. That is where Lusia was living in Brussels, and where she was still living when Dov was born eight months later.


## [HIGH] Notary’s day-book, Tarnów, 19 October 1904 — Mojżesz Rapaport gives his wife Menicha power of attorney to collect the post

**The one light document in the whole archive. Use it early, before the darkness, and let it be funny: a man goes to a notary so that his wife may legally collect the post.**

- Source: Basia, 19-20 August 2026 — Tarnów notarial records; sent as a Word file with the scan embedded, transcribed and translated by Basia
- Scan: https://rapaportfamily.github.io/assets/documents/basia_2026_08_20/tarnow_notary_1904_postal_poa.png
- What it records: entry_number = 392; date = 19 October 1904; office = Deputy of the Imperial-Royal notary in Tarnów, by decree of the Imperial-Royal District Court in Tarnów of 25 July 1904; party = Mojżesz Rapaport, kapitalista (capitalist) in Tarnów; act = Legalizacya podpisu na pełnomocnictwie — legalisation of the signature on a power of attorney dated Tarnów, 19 October; granted_to = his wife, Menicha Rapaportowa (Menycha née Leiner); purpose = do odbioru przesyłek pocztowych — to collect postal items; fee = 2 korony

Nineteen years before the century turned violent, a man walked into a notary’s office in Tarnów and paid two crowns so that his wife could pick up his mail. The clerk wrote entry 392: Mojżesz Rapaport, capitalist in Tarnów, legalises his signature on a power of attorney for his wife Menicha to receive postal items. That is the whole document, and it is worth more than its size. This is Moses Saul Rapaport, born 1858, died in Tarnów on 8 November 1933 — David’s grandfather, Dov’s great-grandfather — and the tree records his occupation as "merchant"; the notary wrote "capitalist". And it is the only document this archive holds that names his wife at all: Menycha Leiner has, until today, been a name on a tree with no birth date, no death date and no paper of her own. Here she is, in 1904, being given legal authority over the family post.

> Still unknown: Why did Moses Saul need a notary for this at all — was he travelling, ill, or simply away on business? The entry does not say.

# Brief for the film — "Dov at Eighty"

**For whoever makes the film, human or LLM. Read this first, then read the manifest.**

- The archive: **https://rapaportfamily.github.io**

Generated 11 August 2026. Runtime 7–10 minutes. Occasion: Dov Bernard Rapaport turns 80 on
28 August 2026.

## Your source is this website and nothing else

Start with the manifest. It holds every chapter with its sources, all **126 documents** with
their decoded fields and file paths, **188 people**, both family trees (**1,442 Rapaports**,
**390 Griffels**), **15 memoir plates**, **120 journey photographs**, **60 unidentified**,
**45 timeline events**, **49 places**, **18 open questions**, and a conflicts list. Read all of
it before writing a word. Anything you cannot trace to it does not go in the film.

## The man this is for

Chronologically eighty; in spirit twenty-eight — energetic, sharp, physically strong, still
arguing. He says one thing repeatedly: he wishes he could take his mother Lusia back to those
places, wheel her there on a gurney if that is what it took, and have her show him where she
lived, what happened, how they survived.

**That wish is the spine of the film.** He cannot take her. So the film does it for him: it goes
to the places, opens the documents, and lays her book beside the evidence.

## Hard rules — not style notes

1. **Never invent.** Every claim traces to the manifest. No invented dialogue, no invented
   dates, no "they must have felt".
2. **Show conflicts as conflicts.** This archive publishes disagreements instead of tidying
   them. Berish's death: two camp records say **29 March 1942**; the Yad Vashem page **David
   himself filed** says 1940. Show both. Same for Rebeka (1888/1892, 1942/1943) and Lotte
   (1915/1914).
3. **The memoir is testimony, not proof — treat it with respect.** Where documents confirm
   Lusia, say so plainly. Where nothing corroborates her, say "no document we hold corroborates
   this" — and immediately say why: the people who would have kept the records are the people
   who were killed. Never imply she was wrong for lacking paper.
4. **Living people: no birth dates.**
5. **Never caption an "unidentified" photograph with a name.** Sixty faces nobody can identify —
   show them as exactly that.
6. **Illustration is welcome but must be visibly marked.** Real photograph and AI illustration
   must never be mistaken for each other.
7. **A document is not a face.** Every portrait in the data was looked at by eye on 19 August
   2026, and forty of the seventy-four "portraits" on the two big trees turned out to be
   passports, qualification certificates, Yad Vashem pages, a gravestone, a Bremen
   missing-persons card. They were demoted. A picture is a person's face only where the record
   carries `photo_verified`. Everything else is a paper about them, and must be captioned as a
   paper about them. The film pack PDF prints verified faces only, so if you work from that file
   you cannot get this wrong.

## The research story — where the film breathes

**Basia** in Poland rebuilt the paternal line back to 1784 and mentioned in passing that the
printed tree is **five metres long**. **Jacob** sent the maternal side — 390 Griffels, and not
one of his 569 files overlapped hers. **Ms. Kasia** at the Jewish Historical Institute in
Warsaw found David's birth certificate and corrected a birthplace a Brussels clerk had misheard
in 1946. And the archive kept catching itself: it filed a 1924 certificate as "a different
family" when the form plainly names Mendel aged 12 and Lotte aged 8; it announced nobody had
remembered Berish and Rebeka while holding the proof; it found two Charlottes born in Vienna a
year apart and nearly merged them.

## Where the humour lives

It must come from the material, never from the tragedy.

- Jacob's folder of forty-seven strangers, named simply **"Photobook2"**.
- Dov photographing Przemyśl **through a car windscreen, in the rain — forty near-identical
  frames**. He was not going to miss it.
- David's destination box: **"Palestine or U.S.A."** A man keeping his options open.
- A five-metre family tree that fits in no room in Israel.
- Dov himself: eighty, and the least eighty-year-old person in the film.

**Never a joke within thirty seconds of Auschwitz, Lwów or the crate.**

## One warning

The manifest's `open_questions` are **questions, not findings** — eighteen of them: how David

invents history. A film that **ends** on them is telling the truth about what this family still
does not know.
