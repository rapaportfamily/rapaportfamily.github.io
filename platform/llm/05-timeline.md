# Timeline (45 events)

- **1888** — Rebeka Griffel born in Nadwórna: Daughter of Leizor Griffel and Sara née Chajes, merchants of Nadwórna.
- **1905** — Elias Weitzner and Matel Weinreb marry in Dolina: Statutory civil marriage in Dolina, Galicia. From Lea's birth certificate.
- **1911** — Feige (Tzipora) Weitzner born in Bolechów: Leah's older sister. Father Elias is listed as a cattle dealer.
- **1911-12-25** — David (Dawid Mendel) Rapaport born in Nadwórna: Son of Berisz Rapaport and Rebeka Griffel. The verified birthplace — the Brussels DP card listing 'Cieszyn (Tesin)' was a transcription error by the French clerk.
- **1912-07-25** — Regina Rapaport registers at Alfredówka in Krynica: Regina (Rebeka) Rapaport, wife of a merchant from Nadwórna, registers alone at the Alfredówka guesthouse in the Krynica resort on 25 July 1912 (newspaper page 7).
- **1913-04-08** — Leah Weitzner born in Bolechów: Daughter of Elias (cattle dealer) and Matel (née Weinreb) Weitzner. Named in Bolechów synagogue on 12 April. Family death certificate lists 6.4.1916 — a conflict to resolve.
- **1916** — Moses (Mojzesz) Weitzner born in Bolechów: Leah's younger brother. Father Elias listed again as cattle dealer.
- **1933** — Weitzner family emigrates to Mandatory Palestine: Elias and Matel sell the tannery and emigrate with three of their four children. Elias obtains a British 'capitalist' visa with 1000 GBP. Leah does NOT join them — she remains in Galicia.
- **1935-02-10** — David Rapaport and Leah Weitzner marry in Bolechów: The civil record gives Bolechów, 10 February 1935 — Lusia 21, David 23. Her memoir places the wedding in Muszyna in February 1938, three years later and in another town; written forty years afterwards, it remembers the marriage as the beginning of the Muszyna years rather than three years before them. The document is followed here; her account is kept beside it.
- **1937-06-22** — Shimon (Szymon) Rapaport born in Lwów: His own post-war survivor card gives 22 June 1937 in Lwów. Family memory had placed his birth in "Mosina" — the town that turned out to be Muszyna — but the card is explicit, and it predates the memoir by forty years.
- **1938-01-01** — David Rapaport in Lwów (1 Jan 1938): Per the 1946 Brussels DP card, David's place of residence on 1 January 1938 was Lwów, Poland.
- **1939** — Leah in Przemyśl, working under Soviet administration: After war broke out, Leah fled to Przemyśl. The city was DIVIDED across the San River — German on one side, Soviet on the other. Leah worked on the Soviet side as a teller and in the passport section. Visited David's parents (Berisz and Rebeka) there.
- **1939-09-01** — World War II begins — Germany invades Poland: Germany invades Poland on 1 Sep 1939. USSR invades from the east on 17 Sep 1939, per the Ribbentrop-Molotov Pact. Eastern Poland (including Lwów, Bolechów, Nadwórna) falls under Soviet occupation.
- **1941** — David escapes the Nadwórna ghetto: David had a work permit to a Nadwórna sawmill (likely the 'essential worker' tag mentioned in family memory). He escaped in a railway wagon — hidden in a niche he carved out between wooden logs — and reached Lwów.
- **1941** — David and Leah in Lwów under false identities: After the Germans took Lwów in 1941, David and Leah survived under fake identities. Leah lived at Legionów Street 24 under 'Hormek' false documents — also used the name 'Maria Cizlik' (a borrowed identity). She managed an art/antiques store. David worked in railway / road construction. They met on 'Rapaport Street' in Lwów. They lived apart for periods. David's sister Lota was also in Lwów.
- **1942** — David stripped of Polish citizenship: DP card notes 'deforce' (deprived) in 1942 — and Kasia (the French expert) read it as 'deported in 1942'. Likely indicates either deportation or formal denaturalization.
- **1946-04-09** — David registered as Displaced Person in Brussels: David Rapaport registered on the DIPIS (Displaced Persons) file at 'B.' (Brussels/Belgium) on 9 April 1946. Listed as married, Polish, Jewish, forestry engineer. Desired destination: Palestine or USA. He signed the card himself. Later stamped by the Belgian Repatriation Commission of the Ministry of Public Health and Family in 1969.
- **1946-08-28** — Dov Bernard Rapaport born in Brussels: Son of David and Leah, brother to Shimon. Born after the war ended.
- **1947-04-02** — David and Shimon sail from Sète on the Theodor Herzl: The ex-British cable-layer HMS Guardian, built of metal in Newcastle in 1907 — 1,768 tons, coal-fired, eight knots flat out — carrying 2,641 ma’apilim gathered from camps in France and Belgium. Her commander was Mecca Limon; the radio operator, the "Gideoni", was Nachman (Bob) Burstein; and a party of journalists sailed with her under Moish Perlman, later head of the Israeli government press office. Lusia stayed behind in Brussels with the eight-month-old Dov.
- **1947-04-13** — The Theodor Herzl is boarded by the Royal Navy: Intercepted off the coast by HMS Haydon and HMS St Brides Bay. The Palyam account calls it one of the most severe battles of the whole Aliyah Bet operation. Three ma’apilim were killed by gunfire and their names are known: AHARON DOV, PINCHAS WEISS and MENACHEM SAMET. The British commanders’ own report reads: "The most unusual feature of the arrest was the level of employment of firearms by the boarding party: of over a score of casualties among the immigrants, ten suffered gunshot wounds, of which two died at the time and one died later." The wounded and the sick were taken to the Atlit detention camp; everyone else was deported to Cyprus. David and nine-year-old Shimon were aboard.
- **1947-08-22** — Shimon released from the Atlit detention camp: The release certificate is printed in his mother’s memoir on page 64 and is dated at Haifa, 22 August 1947: "This is to certify that RAPAPORT SH… whose photograph is hereby attached, has been released from ATHLIT Camp… on authority of the Palestine Government. He/she will be registered as an immigrant to Palestine in due course." He was ten years old. This archive recorded the document as missing and the year as 1948 until August 2026, when the memoir plates were read.
- **1947—1948** — David and Shimon interned at Karaolos, Cyprus: The camps north of Famagusta held 53,510 Jews behind wire between 1946 and 1949, and some two thousand children were born inside them. David and Shimon were there about eight months.
- **1948** — Lusia and Dov sail for Israel on the Kedma: She had stayed in Brussels more than a year with the baby, working, while David and Shimon were behind wire in Cyprus. The Haifa burial register records her year of immigration as 1948 and David’s as 1946 — the two-year gap the memoir describes. Dov’s first journey was with his mother, alone. The memoir names the ship — קדמה, the Kedma — but not the port she sailed from, and gives no date beyond the year. - **1948** — The family is together again in Haifa: Lusia and Dov reach Haifa and are reunited with her parents, who had left for Palestine fifteen years earlier and whom she had not seen since. They went from the ship straight to her parents’ small flat on Shalom Street; the family later lived on Abbas Street, and later still on Moriah Street.
- **1948-05-14** — The State of Israel is declared: The Cyprus detainees were released to Israel over the months that followed.
- **1950s** — The apartment on Moriah Street, bought with German reparations: David worked as a customs broker in Haifa — the trade his son Dov, and later his grandson Doron, would follow.
- **1958** — David and Lusia travel to West Berlin over the reparations: The memoir records David wrestling with it: part of him could not forgive the Germans, and part of him argued it would be wrong to refuse what was lawfully owed. The memoir does not date the trip; the late 1950s is our estimate.
- **1967-04-17** — Shimon marries Tamar (Tami) Bugin in Tel Aviv: Shimon — the boy who spent two and a half years in a wooden crate in Lwów — became a journalist, working for Ma’ariv from the Tiberias and Haifa bureaus.
- **1975-05-16** — Dov marries Dalia in Haifa: Read from the ketubah itself on 2 August 2026: "here, in the holy community of Haifa, may it be rebuilt speedily in our days" — the place is written into the document. The year is 5735; the groom is Dov son of Menachem David, the bride Dalia daughter of Dov Yosef. A machine reading in June 2026 had given Brussels and flagged an oddity, that the document said "on Shabbat" while the date fell on a Friday. Both were errors of reading rather than of the scribe: the printed formula is "on the [Nth] day of the week", and filled in for Friday it reads exactly as it should. The compiled family tree’s 16 January 1974 in Haifa had the town right and the date wrong.
- **1986** — Lusia tells her story — the memoir is recorded: It began with a school assignment: her granddaughter Hadas approached her teacher, Esther Weiss, who then interviewed Lusia. Ninety pages in Hebrew. Without them almost nothing in this archive between 1939 and 1948 would have a voice.
- **1990-08-29** — David (Memek) Rapaport dies in Haifa: 8 Elul 5750. Buried at Sde Yehoshua cemetery, section ו, row 22, grave 102ד. He was 78. His death notice ran in Ma’ariv the following day.
- **1996-12-28** — Leah (Lusia) Rapaport dies in Haifa: 18 Tevet 5757. Buried beside David at Sde Yehoshua, grave 102ג — the two graves adjacent. Her burial record names her father Eliyahu and her mother Matilda, the same parents her memoir opens with. She was 83.
