# Requesting the German compensation (BEG) files — David and Lusia Rapaport

**Prepared 3 August 2026 for Doron Rapaport to send.** Free of charge.

## Why we know there is a file

This is not a guess. Lusia's memoir records two things plainly:

- David and Lusia **travelled to West Berlin over the reparations**, around 1958, and the memoir
  describes David arguing with himself about whether it was right to accept German money at all.
- The **apartment on Moriah Street in Haifa was bought with the proceeds**.

A claim that was pursued in person in West Berlin and paid out means a file exists — a
*Bundesentschädigungsgesetz* (BEG) case file, opened by the Berlin compensation authority. Those
files are typically thick: the claimant's own account of what was done to them, medical
assessments, witness statements from other survivors, and the authority's correspondence. For a
family whose entire wartime record is one DP card and a memoir, a BEG file could be the single
largest surviving body of David's and Lusia's own testimony.

## Where to write

**1. To find the file number, if it is not known**

> BEG Central Register (Bundeszentralkartei)
> Bezirksregierung Düsseldorf, Dezernat 15
> Postfach 30 08 65
> D-40408 Düsseldorf, Germany

They hold the central index of BEG claimants and can tell you **which authority holds the file and
its reference number**.

**2. The Berlin authority — the likely holder, given the Berlin trip**

> Landesamt für Bürger- und Ordnungsangelegenheiten (LABO)
> Entschädigungsbehörde
> Fehrbelliner Platz 1
> 10702 Berlin, Germany
> **Entschaedigung@labo.berlin.de** · +49 30 90269-5140

## What to ask for

Ask for **archival access to the case file as an heir**, not for a new claim. This distinction
matters: new BEG claims closed long ago and required the victim to be living, but the **files
themselves are archival records** and descendants can request copies. Say plainly that you are the
grandson, that both claimants are deceased, and that you are assembling a family archive.

### Notes for whoever sends this

- Write in German if you can; English is accepted. Say **"Akteneinsicht als Erbe"** — archival
  access as an heir.
- Include a copy of your own ID and something showing descent (Dov's birth record, or the burial
  records naming David and Lusia).
- Expect months, not weeks. Berlin holds roughly a million of these files.
- **If a file comes back, it belongs in `documents.json` and on the Research Center page** — not
  only in an inbox. That is the standing rule in this archive.
