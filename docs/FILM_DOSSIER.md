### Before the beginning — Radomyśl Wielki  ·  1850—1886

- For a year this family could go back no further than a name on a passport. The paternal line stopped at Berisz Rapaport, and even Edward Gelles, who spent twenty-five years on the Rapaport rabbinical genealogies, could not carry it past him. In May 2026 Basia found the passport file itself, and inside it were his parents: Moses Saul Rapaport and Menukha.
  <br>*Berisz Rapaport passport application, Stanisławów 1924*
- Berisz was born on 30 July 1886 in Radomyśl Wielki, and the family's registered commune was Dąbrowa Tarnowska — a town nobody could account for until July 2026, when Basia read the Tarnów notarial records and found Szabse Rapaport serving as rabbi of Dąbrowa until 20 April 1884. That is why the passport says Dąbrowa. The same evening, from a completely different direction, Dana was sent a record naming Schabse with Figa/Yete.
  <br>*Tarnów notarial records (Basia, July 2026) · MyHeritage record, 7 July 2026*
- Three marriage deeds from Tarnów — 1919, 1920 and 1923, for three of Berisz's sisters — each independently name the same couple as their parents. Three separate clerks, in three different years, writing the same two names. That is as close to certainty as this kind of research reaches.
  <br>*Tarnów marriage registers 1919 (entry 40), 1920 (entry 98), 1923 (entry 101)*

### Nadwórna — David  ·  1911

- David Mendel Rapaport — Memek — was born in Nadwórna on 25 December 1911, son of Berisz Rapaport and Rebeka Griffel. Nadwórna sat in the Carpathian foothills of eastern Galicia, then Austria-Hungary, then Poland, today Nadvirna in Ukraine.
  <br>*Nadwórna civil birth register*
- **⚠ CONFLICT — do not resolve this for the audience.** His Brussels Displaced Persons card of 1946 gives his birthplace as "Cieszyn (Tesin)" — a town four hundred kilometres away, where no one in this family ever lived. It is a transcription error by a French clerk, and it is worth pausing on: here the document is wrong and the family's memory is right. That is why nothing in this archive ranks paper above testimony as a rule.
  <br>*DIPIS card, Brussels 1946 · Nadwórna civil birth register*

### Bolechów — Lusia  ·  1913—1933

### The war  ·  1939—1944

- **◆ TESTIMONY ONLY — no document. Show it as remembered.** Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939. What the family did over the next five years is known almost entirely from Lusia's testimony, because almost no one who could have written it down survived to do so.
  <br>*Memoir chs.2-6*
- **◆ TESTIMONY ONLY — no document. Show it as remembered.** She tells of Przemyśl under Soviet administration; of the Nadwórna ghetto and David's escape from it hidden in a wooden wagon; of false identities in Lwów; of the Hormak family, and of neighbours named Pietrozycki and Ivanov who helped them; and of Shimon, three years old, kept in a crate for two and a half years so that he would not be seen or heard. No document we hold corroborates any of it. That is not a weakness in the account — it is what happens when the people who would have kept the records are the people who were killed.
  <br>*Memoir chs.3-6 — no corroborating record held*
- Two things from those years are on paper. David was stripped of Polish citizenship in 1942. And his father Berisz was murdered at Auschwitz — our research associates him with victim number 188161, a reading we have asked the Arolsen Archives to confirm or correct. No Page of Testimony was ever filed at Yad Vashem for Berisz or for Rebeka. This archive is, at present, their only written memorial anywhere.
  <br>*DIPIS card, Brussels 1946 · Yad Vashem name search, 21 May 2026 — nil result*

### Katowice — and a separation nobody recorded  ·  1945—1946

- The war ended and they came west with the repatriation, to Katowice. On 1 July 1946 Lusia and Shimon each filed a survivor-registration card at the Katowice committee — cards 151738 and 151337, mother and son, one after the other.
  <br>*CKŻP cards nr 151738 and nr 151337, Katowice, 1 July 1946*
- **⚠ CONFLICT — do not resolve this for the audience.** But David had been registered as a Displaced Person in Brussels since 9 April 1946 — three months earlier. On the day he was recorded in Belgium, his wife and son were still in Poland. He had gone ahead, and they followed later that year. The memoir says only that "the family reaches Brussels and stays in a hotel": one movement, everyone together. The two cards, held in this archive since May and never read against each other until August 2026, say otherwise. It is the first of the two separations in this story.
  <br>*DIPIS card, 9 April 1946 · CKŻP cards, 1 July 1946 · Memoir p.62*

### Brussels — and Dov  ·  1946

- David's Displaced Persons card describes a forestry engineer, Polish nationality stripped in 1942, speaking Polish, Italian, German, Czech and Russian. It is the only document that reaches across from the war into what came after.
  <br>*DIPIS card, Brussels, 9 April 1946*
- Dov Bernard Rapaport was born in Brussels on 28 August 1946. No Belgian birth registration is held in this archive — it is the easiest missing document of all, and only he can request it, as the person the record belongs to.
  <br>*Family (Doron, 2026) · Memoir p.62*

### Sète, the Theodor Herzl, Cyprus  ·  1947—1948

- On 2 April 1947 David and Shimon sailed from Sète in southern France aboard the Theodor Herzl — the ex-British cable-layer HMS Guardian, built at Newcastle in 1907, carrying 2,641 ma'apilim gathered from camps across France and Belgium. Shimon was nine. Lusia stayed behind in Brussels with the seven-month-old Dov.
  <br>*Memoir p.63 · Palyam / ship histories — the vessel is fully documented*
- On 13 April the Royal Navy boarded her off the coast. The passengers fought; three were killed and twenty-seven wounded. Everyone aboard was deported to Cyprus, and David and Shimon were interned at Karaolos, north of Famagusta, for about eight months. The camps there held 53,510 Jews behind wire between 1946 and 1949, and some two thousand children were born inside them. Shimon was released from Atlit in 1948.
  <br>*Memoir pp.63-66 · Cyprus internment camp figures, 1946-1949*

### Haifa  ·  1948—1996

- Lusia sailed from Brussels with Dov on the Kadima. She had waited more than a year alone with the baby, working, while David and Shimon were behind wire. The Haifa burial register records her year of immigration as 1948 and David's as 1946 — the two-year gap the memoir describes, written into their own graves. Dov's first journey was with his mother, the two of them.
  <br>*Memoir p.66 · Haifa Chevra Kadisha register*
- **◆ TESTIMONY ONLY — no document. Show it as remembered.** In Haifa she saw her parents for the first time since 1933. The family settled on Abbas Street, and later bought an apartment on Moriah Street with German reparations — money David argued with himself about taking. He worked as a customs broker, the trade his son Dov followed, and then his grandson Doron.
  <br>*Memoir pp.67-76*
- In 1986 a granddaughter, Hadas, asked her teacher Esther Weiss for help with a school assignment, and Esther sat down and interviewed Lusia. Ninety pages in Hebrew. Without them, almost nothing in this archive between 1939 and 1948 would have a voice at all.
  <br>*The memoir, compiled 1986*
- David died on 29 August 1990 and Lusia on 28 December 1996. They are buried beside one another at Sde Yehoshua, graves 102ד and 102ג. Her burial record names her father Eliyahu and her mother Matilda — the same two people her memoir opens with, sixty years earlier and in another language. The last page of the record matches the first page of the testimony.
  <br>*Haifa Chevra Kadisha register · Memoir ch.1*
